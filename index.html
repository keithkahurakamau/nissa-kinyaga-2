01 The Story

Born of the forest,
raised by the wild.

I am Nissa Ole Kinyaga, of the Maasai. I grew up in the Mukogodo Forest of northern Kenya, where I learned to read the land long before I ever called it a profession, the alarm call of a bird, the freshness of a track, the direction of the wind.

My path into guiding began as a radio signaller at the Lewa Wildlife Conservancy. From there I enrolled at the Kenya Utalii College for an advanced tour-guiding certificate, and went on to study advanced ornithology, walking safaris, astronomy and bush first aid. In 2002, I became a full-time safari guide, and I have never stopped learning since.

Today I guide at Lengishu, a private home in the heart of the 32,000-acre Borana Conservancy, one of Kenya's most successful rhino sanctuaries. My days are spent among leopards and wild dogs, rhino and elephant, in rough mountainous terrain laced with deep valleys and riverine forest.

"Every day I am thankful to be surrounded by such beauty, the rough mountainous terrain with its deep valleys and riverine."

Nissa, interview with Carrier / Luxury London, 2021

04 My Safari Philosophy

A safari should leave
the wild better than it found it.

Respect before the sighting

The animal's space comes first. The best encounters happen when the wild forgets we are there at all.

Conservation you can see

Revenue from tourism on Borana goes back to local communities and their livelihoods, protecting these animals for future generations.

Travel that gives back

In the evenings, guests can join the anti-poaching rangers on a drive, sharing stories, and understanding their way of life.

Knowledge for the young

I love sharing success stories with younger generations. Conservation only lasts if it is passed on.

"Always be honest, help others, travel sustainably, and share the importance of conservation with younger generations as much as possible."

Nissa Ole Kinyaga
